# Ethical Commerce Charter (working draft)

1. **No ethical-washing.** Label statements as unverified, supplier-declared, checked evidence or independently verified. Attach sources, dates, and known gaps.
2. **Human rights first.** Serious indicators of forced labour, unsafe workplaces or child exploitation require restrictions, protective escalation and remediation. Good environmental performance cannot cancel human-rights risks.
3. **Independent accountability.** Merchants cannot self-certify independent verification. Independent specialists and qualified assessors are needed for relevant assurance.
4. **Truthful costs.** No drip pricing, fake scarcity, paid disguised rankings, fabricated reviews or invented delivery commitments.
5. **Consumer rights.** Provide required information on the legal seller, cancellations, refunds, warranties and complaint routes before transactions are enabled.
6. **Worker welfare.** Where practical, use locally meaningful living-income/wage benchmarks with source and methodology; never treat minimum wage compliance as proof of fairness.
7. **Environment.** Lifecycle calculations disclose scope, boundaries, assumptions, methodology, estimates and limitations. Unsupported blanket claims are disallowed.
8. **Animal welfare.** Apply relevant standards to category-specific claims; mark evidence status.
9. **Repair and circularity.** Surface repairs, spare parts, refurbished and second-hand alternatives.
10. **Privacy and accessibility.** Data minimisation, accessible UI, retention rules and opt-in choices.
11. **Protected reporting.** Sensitive reports require restricted access and specialised handling, not public display.
12. **Fair enforcement.** Written policies, proportional sanctions, appeals, remediation timelines and public aggregate reporting.

**IMPORTANT:** Claims and evidence currently remain supplier-submitted until an independent verification workflow is operational. Supplier verification and external audits can have real costs.
